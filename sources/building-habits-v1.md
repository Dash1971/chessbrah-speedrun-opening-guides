## Representative teaching moments

### Series method

- [Part 1, 0:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=14s) — Aman explains the rule-based format, accepts that stronger moves may be missed, and prioritizes fundamentals and high-percentage moves.
- [Part 1, 43:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=2594s) — the first four restrictions together: no premoves, tactics, gambits, or sacrifices.
- [Part 1, 43:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=2609s) — later skills are added to the initial rules rather than replacing the foundation.
- [Part 9, 0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=45s) — ratings are not rigid cutoffs; add the next rules when the first set has taken the player to the next level.

### Level 1: roughly 400–700

- [Part 1, 0:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=49s) — start with `e4` and control the centre.
- [Part 1, 1:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=99s) — at the first level, take available exchanges instead of calculating long variations.
- [Part 1, 3:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=190s) — castle instead of chasing uncertain opportunities.
- [Part 1, 3:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=212s) — why tactics are postponed rather than condemned.
- [Part 1, 4:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=245s) — bring the rooks toward the middle after the other pieces are developed.
- [Part 1, 44:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=2643s) — develop and operate with knights before bishops as the first shortcut.
- [Part 1, 56:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=3399s) — create an escape square for the castled king.
- [Part 1, 1:46:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=6417s) — why premoves are unnecessary at 400.
- [Part 1, 1:47:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8pZbhjL-fQ&t=6427s) — random pawn moves come only after the planned development is complete.
- [Part 2, 36:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZIuV0_gzgzc&t=2216s) — use the king and attack pawns in the endgame.
- [Part 3, 47:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SmxnZQITf28&t=2834s) — push a passed pawn.
- [Part 3, 54:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SmxnZQITf28&t=3246s) — take an equal-value trade under the early rule.
- [Part 5, 1:25:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zDKC62tWFEw&t=5107s) — kick an exposed bishop with the nearby rook pawn.

### Level 2: roughly 700–1100

- [Part 9, 1:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=65s) — overview of the Level 2 changes.
- [Part 9, 1:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=109s) — safe premoved recaptures; broader premoves only below ten seconds.
- [Part 9, 2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=153s) — basic forks, pins, skewers, discoveries, and one-move mates are introduced.
- [Part 9, 3:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=224s) — castle quickly, but no longer necessarily as soon as legally possible.
- [Part 9, 4:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=248s) — rook behind passed pawns.
- [Part 9, 4:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=258s) — keep the tension in a pin.
- [Part 9, 4:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=270s) — use more aggressive development and try to occupy the centre with both pawns.
- [Part 9, 5:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=349s) — the recurring Ruy Lopez exception where `...dxc6` captures away from the centre.
- [Part 9, 14:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rf7V3MCOum4&t=844s) — bishop-for-knight exchanges must be evaluated rather than made automatically.
- [Part 11, 21:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dZ4iVUQQ4T8&t=1292s) — never resign; require the opponent to finish the game.

### Level 3: roughly 1100–1550

- [Part 17, 0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=45s) — Level 3 means becoming proactive, choosing consciously, and reducing autopilot.
- [Part 17, 1:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=100s) — put pieces on the best available squares; a rook may belong on an open file rather than in the centre.
- [Part 17, 3:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=184s) — study recurring opening positions after the game and commit a small answer to memory.
- [Part 17, 4:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=243s) — exact basic pawn and rook endings become memorized knowledge.
- [Part 17, 5:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=330s) — never resign remains; gambits and sacrifices remain outside the training set.
- [Part 17, 6:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=408s) — retain tension and take most, not all, trades.
- [Part 17, 13:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=802s) — the first opening-specific Level 3 lesson: the Two Knights against the Caro-Kann.
- [Part 17, 17:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=1020s) — opening theory is a remembered phase; afterward, return to the habits.
- [Part 17, 1:08:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7u8KXf-KzEE&t=4089s) — Scandinavian: bring the opponent's queen out and develop against it with tempo.
- [Part 18, 51:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tOsISkmNwpg&t=3110s) — begin a dedicated answer to the frequently encountered London.
- [Part 18, 1:29:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tOsISkmNwpg&t=5371s) — first French game and the decision to establish a repeatable system in review.
- [Part 19, 47:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qK0Ds5vLAjE&t=2854s) — Sicilian: `c3` with the intention of `d4`.

### Level 4: roughly 1550–1900

- [Part 27, 0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=45s) — the remaining training wheels begin to come off.
- [Part 27, 1:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=69s) — sacrifices are introduced.
- [Part 27, 1:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=109s) — hard prohibitions such as never playing `g4` are lifted; benefits and weaknesses must be compared.
- [Part 27, 2:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=177s) — pawn structures, isolated pawns, backward pawns, and other weaknesses become central.
- [Part 27, 3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=219s) — Philidor, Lucena, opposition, pawn endings, and rook endings are required knowledge.
- [Part 27, 5:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=310s) — choose rook squares consciously; use open files rather than a blanket centre rule.
- [Part 27, 9:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=563s) — gambits are now allowed, although the established repertoire need not be discarded.
- [Part 27, 3:10:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x82SIL6XCHI&t=11431s) — the Nimzo-Indian is introduced specifically to teach pawn structures and bishop-knight imbalances.
- [Part 29, 0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TISxvO2dr1c&t=45s) — Ruy Lopez, colour complexes, weak pawns, imbalances, and endgames define the later opening work.
- [Part 29, 1:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TISxvO2dr1c&t=110s) — identify d5 as a permanent weakness and build every piece placement around it.
- [Part 29, 3:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TISxvO2dr1c&t=198s) — occupy the outpost after removing the bishop that controlled it.
- [Part 29, 52:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TISxvO2dr1c&t=3160s) — another explicit outpost lesson.
