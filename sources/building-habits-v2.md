## Representative teaching moments

### Level 1: approximately 400–700

- [Episode 1, 0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COuBww22MUA&t=0s) — the first game begins with the central pawn and simple development routine.
- [Episode 1, 54:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COuBww22MUA&t=3283s) — never resign; no premoves, tactics, gambits, or sacrifices.
- [Episode 2, 18:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dptb79elO04&t=1123s) — RPM is named as Random Pawn Move.
- [Episode 2, 47:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dptb79elO04&t=2873s) — RPM answers the “what do I do now?” middlegame problem after the setup is complete.
- [Episode 3, 3:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=txtjq9AhNbE&t=215s) — `h3` or `...h6` is introduced as the snorkel.
- [Episode 3, 41:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=txtjq9AhNbE&t=2501s) — centre pawn, development, castle, then snorkel.
- [Episode 4, 1:27:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WyfYv36M8Qc&t=5244s) — the first full red-dot explanation: move the king off the dangerous line.
- [Episode 4, 2:00:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WyfYv36M8Qc&t=7242s) — never resign because opponents still fail to convert or stalemate.
- [Episode 5, 2:03:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aSyCrQVVGU4&t=7421s) — use the king in the endgame to attack pawns.
- [Episode 5, 2:49:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aSyCrQVVGU4&t=10151s) — use the king as a weapon.
- [Episode 5, 3:21:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aSyCrQVVGU4&t=12063s) — the red-dot side-step is restated as a compact habit.
- [Episode 5, 3:23:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aSyCrQVVGU4&t=12184s) — explicit definition of RPM.
- [Episode 6, 1:13:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yNPv5lrbHDk&t=4414s) — the snorkel can wait when its timing creates a problem.
- [Episode 6, 1:39:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yNPv5lrbHDk&t=5980s) — prefer a central RPM when one is available.

### Level 3: approximately 1100–1600

- [Episode 25, 0:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O4sskxURwtk&t=59s) — the biggest change is opening move order, not a new set of moves.

- [Episode 34, 1:13:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WkvqIGsvzHU&t=4424s) — only prepared opening sacrifices are allowed; do not invent speculative sacrifices.

## PGN provenance and reconciliation

The published PGN combines two compatible sources:

- the supplied 527-game V2 collection, including its clock comments, teaching notes, and five analysis variations;
- the independently verified corpus headers, which add Chess.com game links, opening names, episode numbers, VOD timestamps, and link precision.

Player order, result, and every played main-line move agree in 526 games. In game 311, the supplied file continued after `10.Bb5` with a line labelled “A possible continuation,” while the verified Chess.com score ended at `10.Bb5` when Black resigned. The published PGN preserves the actual ten-move game and records the supplied continuation in a clearly labelled analysis note.

Of the 527 games:

- 413 have VOD timestamps corroborated by an independent board-position match;
- 102 have timestamps derived from the solved VOD time base;
- 12 were played by the account between stream windows and are labelled `not-on-stream` rather than being assigned a false episode.

## Community comparison supplied for review

The discussion was used as a comparison checklist, not as authority. It correctly pointed toward the red dot, the stronger RPM terminology, the absence of a formal rook-file lesson, earlier bishop-versus-knight discussion, and the more gradual progression.
