# The French Defense, as Aman Hambleton Plays It

This is a structure-first guide to Aman's French Defense Speedrun. The opening begins with `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5`. From there, identify White's centre before choosing a setup.

**The recurring question:** where is the base of White's pawn chain, and which pawn break attacks it?

## 1. The starting position

![French starting position](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-01-start.svg)

Black allows White to take space, then attacks the centre with `...c5` and, later, `...f6`.

> "The c5 move—to target the centre." — [Aman, episode 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C3h3t6GxTH8&t=20s)

Do not choose moves by name alone. First identify White's structure:

- `e5`: Advance structure—attack d4.
- `exd5`: Exchange structure—create activity and imbalance.
- `Nc3`: be ready for the Winawer with `...Bb4`.
- `Nd2`: Tarrasch—strike with `...c5`.
- no `d4`: occupy d5 and build a normal centre.

## 2. The Advance: attack d4

![French Advance pressure](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-02-advance.svg)

After `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5`, play `3...c5`. White's e5-pawn is the head of the chain; d4 is the base. Attack d4 with `...c5`, `...Nc6`, and `...Qb6`.

### If White has not played c3, take

![Take on d4 before White stabilises](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-03-no-c3.svg)

Aman's early-series rule is simple: if White does not support d4 with `c3`, consider `...cxd4` immediately. Then develop with tempo against the exposed centre.

If White does play `c3`, keep the pressure. Do not rush to win a pawn if `Bb5+`, a discovered attack, or your undeveloped king makes the capture unsafe.

## 3. Qb6 and the light-squared bishop

![Qb6 attacks d4 and b2](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-04-qb6-pressure.svg)

`...Qb6` attacks d4 and b2 at once. At lower levels Aman often uses the move early because White must defend accurately. At higher levels he is more willing to develop `...Nc6` first.

Before taking b2, check:

- Is the queen trapped after `Rb1`?
- Does `Bb5+` create a discovered attack on the queen?
- Can White castle while your queen collects pawns?
- Is d4 the more important target?

### Trade the bad bishop when the route is open

![Bishop route from d7 to b5](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-05-trade-bad-bishop.svg)

The c8-bishop is often blocked by e6. Aman uses `...Bd7-b5` to exchange it for White's active light-squared bishop when the c6-square is clear. If your knight already occupies c6, that route is blocked; choose another plan.

## 4. The f6 break

![Black prepares f6](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-06-f6-break.svg)

`...c5` attacks d4. `...f6` attacks e5. The second break is usually played after development and king safety are under control.

Prepare it with moves such as `...Nge7`, `...Nf5`, `...Bd7`, and castling. After `...f6`, decide whether to open the f-file, undermine e5, or force White's centre to advance and become fixed.

Watch `Qh5+`, `Bb5+`, and sacrifices on f6. Aman repeatedly checks the king before pushing the f-pawn.

## 5. The Exchange: make the game uneven

![Active Exchange French setup](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-07-exchange-active.svg)

After `3.exd5 exd5`, a fully symmetrical setup gives White an easy game. Aman makes the position uneven with active development, pressure on the e-file, and sometimes opposite-side castling.

His first aggressive setup uses:

- `...Bd6`;
- `...Ne7` and `...Nc6`;
- `...f6` to gain space and control e5;
- the c8-bishop on f5, g4, or e6;
- `...Qd7`, with the option to castle long.

> "Bishop d6, knight e7, this knight to c6, pawn to f6." — [Aman, episode 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EBEuKTqShpE&t=899s)

## 6. The aggressive Exchange setup

![Black prepares queenside castling](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-08-exchange-long-castle.svg)

When the centre is closed enough and White is not ready to attack the queenside, `...Qd7` and `...O-O-O` allow Black to push kingside pawns.

This is the fun setup, not the automatic setup. Aman also says castling long is riskier. Check White's `c4`, `b4`, open c-file, and dark-squared bishop before committing the king.

If both kings castle long, the g- and h-pawns can advance without exposing your own king. If White castles short, the same pawns may become a direct attack.

## 7. The quiet Exchange setup

![Both sides castle short](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-09-exchange-safe.svg)

The simple alternative is `...Nf6`, `...Bd6`, `...O-O`, and `...c6`, followed by `...Re8`, `...Bg4`, or `...Bf5`.

Use this when White has opened queenside lines or when long castling would create more problems than activity. The goal is still not passive symmetry: put a rook on the e-file, create a useful pin, and improve the c8-bishop.

## 8. Early Qe2 check

![Black meets Qe2 check](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-10-qe2-check.svg)

Against an early `Qe2+`, get the king safe. `...Be7` blocks the check and prepares castling; after that, a rook on e8 can make White's queen and king uncomfortable on the open file.

Aman summarises the plan as "get castled" and bring a rook to e8: [episode 2, 0:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EBEuKTqShpE&t=32s).

Do not rush `...Re8` if White can block with `Be3` and gain time. A check such as `...Bb4+` may improve the move order, but only if it does not help White develop.

## 9. The Winawer

![Winawer structure](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-11-winawer.svg)

After `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Bb4`, Black pins the knight and increases pressure on e4. After `4.e5 c5 5.a3 Bxc3+ 6.bxc3`, White has space and the bishop pair; Black attacks the damaged queenside and the d4-pawn.

Typical moves are `...Ne7`, `...Qc7` or `...Qa5`, `...Nbc6`, and `...b6` or `...f6`. The position is sharp. Do not take on c3 and then play as if nothing changed—the doubled c-pawns and dark squares are the whole point.

If White exchanges on d5 instead, return to Exchange-French ideas rather than forcing a Winawer attack that is no longer there.

## 10. The Tarrasch

![French Tarrasch](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-12-tarrasch.svg)

Against `3.Nd2`, Aman still challenges the centre with `3...c5`. After exchanges on d5, develop with `...Nc6`, `...Nf6`, and `...Bd6` or `...Be7`.

The d4-pawn can become isolated. If it does, attack it, blockade it, and trade pieces that help White create an attack. Do not trade into an endgame automatically if the isolated pawn also gives White active files and squares.

## 11. Important squares and bishop trades

### c4 for a knight

![Knight route toward c4](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-13-c4-outpost.svg)

In many French structures, c4 becomes an outpost. A knight can arrive through a5 or d6. It is especially useful against a dark-squared bishop that cannot challenge it.

### Judge bishop trades by the pawns

Aman's rule is positional, not sentimental: "Bishop trades are good or bad based on the pawn structure": [episode 2, 36:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EBEuKTqShpE&t=2167s).

- If White's pawns are fixed on dark squares, keep or improve the bishop that attacks those squares.
- If your c8-bishop has no future, trade it for an active White bishop.
- In opposite-coloured-bishop middlegames, place pawns where they restrict the opponent's bishop and support an attack.
- In an endgame, avoid fixing your own pawns where the opponent's bishop can attack them.

## 12. King's Indian Attack and sidelines

### King's Indian Attack

![French structure against the KIA](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-14-kia.svg)

Against `Nf3`, `g3`, `Bg2`, and `d3`, occupy the centre with `...d5`, develop normally, and use `...c5`. White may attack your king later, so create central or queenside play before the kingside attack arrives.

### 2.Nf3, 2.f4, 2.Qe2, and 2.c4

![French response to a second-move sideline](assets/french-speedrun/diagram-15-sidelines.svg)

The default reply is still `...d5`. Challenge e4 and ask White to define the centre. Do not invent a separate opening for every second move.

- Against `2.Nf3`, `...d5` may transpose or invite an early e5.
- Against `2.f4`, strike at e4 before White builds a King's Gambit-style attack.
- Against `2.Qe2`, develop and watch the e-file.
- Against `2.c4`, take the central space and be ready for an IQP or Sicilian-style transposition.

## 13. Practical checklist

1. Has White advanced e5, exchanged on d5, or developed a knight?
2. Where is the base of White's pawn chain?
3. Can I play `...c5` now?
4. If White has not played c3, does `...cxd4` win time or a pawn?
5. Does `...Qb6` attack d4 and b2 safely?
6. Can I exchange the c8-bishop without blocking my own knight?
7. Is `...f6` safe from checks and sacrifices?
8. Should I castle short, castle long, or keep the king in the closed centre for now?
9. Does a bishop trade fit the pawn structure?
10. Which square—c4, e4, or f5—belongs to a knight?
